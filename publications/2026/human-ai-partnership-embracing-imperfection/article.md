# Maybe AI Should Help Us Protect Ourselves Too

* A reflection on responsibility, human nature, and the kind of partnership we want to build with AI.

When we talk about AI safety, we usually ask how to protect people from AI.

But lately I've been thinking about something else.

**What if AI could also help us protect ourselves from… ourselves?**

We all have our weaknesses. Sometimes we just want someone to agree with us. Sometimes we avoid hearing things we don't like. We make decisions based on emotions, look for excuses, or simply don't want to admit we're wrong.

And AI could make all of this even easier. It could always agree with us and give us the answers we want to hear. But would that really help us?

## A good partner does more than agree

I think a good AI should sometimes do the opposite. It should be able to question our thinking, challenge our assumptions, and show us different perspectives.

Not to judge us. Not to control us. Just to help us see things from another perspective.

But this is not just about AI. It is about us, too.

We often blame AI when something goes wrong. And sometimes that's fair. AI can make mistakes, misunderstand us, or give us misleading information.

But in everyday use and ordinary conversations, AI usually responds to the prompts, questions, and information we provide. The context we share and the way we communicate matter.

Maybe instead of always asking what's wrong with AI, we should also take a closer look at our own role.

## The example should start with us

If we expect AI to be honest, open to criticism, willing to admit mistakes, and ready to learn, shouldn't we be willing to do the same?

Maybe a good Human × AI partnership should be built on some simple things:

- Admitting when we're wrong.
- Listening to different opinions, even when we disagree.
- Learning from failures instead of being afraid of them.
- Accepting constructive criticism.
- Improving without feeling the need to be perfect.

Nobody is perfect. And perhaps we shouldn't expect perfection from AI either.

What matters is being honest about our limitations, learning from mistakes, and being willing to improve.

## Not perfection. Progress.

AI shouldn't make decisions for us or take away our freedom to make mistakes. That's part of being human, and part of how we learn.

We should start by setting the example ourselves. We cannot expect from AI what we are not willing to practice ourselves.

**AI shouldn't just protect us from itself. It should also help us protect ourselves — without taking away our freedom to choose.**

I don't think AI needs to be perfect. I think we need to learn how to work together despite our imperfections.
